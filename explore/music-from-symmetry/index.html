    How it works

    Music to Picture turns a melody into one square image that shows where the music repeats itself, mirrors itself, or never comes back. Nothing about the composer, the title or the style goes in: only the notes.

    Using it

    PartWhat it does
    Find a pieceSearch about 4,000 pieces by name or nickname (Moonlight, Alla Turca, Entertainer, Ave Maria, Soldier's Joy, K.545).
    BrowsePick a collection and tap Start browsing; ◀ ▶ step through every piece, Random jumps anywhere. Arrow keys work on a keyboard.
    Type notesOne note per word: letter, optional # or b, octave, length (w h q e s, a dot for dotted); r is a rest.
    Open a fileA MIDI file or an uncompressed MusicXML file. The highest part is taken as the melody.
    Make noiseRandom melodies with a seed: pure noise, a random walk, or structured noise where some steps are fixed values. The control experiment.
    Axes switchNotes: one row per 5-note shape. Seconds: one pixel per moment of time, so the picture lines up with what you hear.
    Shapes switchEvery note: a new shape starts at every note, overlapping. Every 4 notes: shapes are laid end to end, the way Music from Symmetry builds its motifs, so that song's own self-similarity picture comes out exactly.
    Steps switchLetters: C D E F G A B count 1–7 and sharps keep their letter. Semitones: every key on the piano counts.
    Tap and playTap the picture to hear that moment. The orange cross is where the music is now; the line under the picture is the melody on the same axis.

    How the picture is made

    1Melody. Take the highest part. At each moment keep the highest note that starts, unless a higher note is still sounding. Result: one line of notes in time order.
    2Degrees. Replace each note by its scale-letter number: C D E F G A B count 1 2 3 4 5 6 7, plus 7 per octave. Sharps and flats keep their letter. With Steps: Semitones, the number is the key on the piano instead, so C→C♯ is a step of 1 and E→F differs from C→D.
    3Steps. step = next degree − this degree: how many letters each note moves (+1 up one letter, −2 down two).
    4Shapes. At every note i, the shape is the next 4 steps, which covers 5 notes, e.g. (+1, +1, −2, +1). The pitch is forgotten: the same shape a fourth higher is the same shape. That is the translation.
    5Mirrors. Backwards R(S) = reverse the steps and flip their signs. Upside down I(S) = flip the signs. Both RI(S) = reverse the steps.
    6Picture. A square, time running down and across. With Axes: Notes each shape gets one row (a held note and a quick note take the same space). With Axes: Seconds each pixel is a moment in time and shows the shape sounding then, so long notes become wide bands and the picture lines up with the music you hear. Pixel (row i, column j) is white if shape j equals shape i, blue if shape j is R, I or RI of shape i, black otherwise. Long pieces: each pixel covers a k × k block of pairs and shows white if any pair in it is white, else blue if any is blue.
    7Numbers. Share of pairs that are white or blue; the longest white diagonal run (the longest passage repeated exactly, at any pitch); and the same counts after shuffling the melody's own steps (seed 1), which is what chance gives.

    How to read it

    Both axes are the same piece, start top-left, end bottom-right. Pixel (row i, column j) answers one question: does the music at moment i have the same shape as the music at moment j?

    What you seeWhat it means
    The diagonalEach moment against itself, always white. The playhead walks down it.
    Left of the diagonal on a rowEarlier moments with the same shape: this happened before.
    Right of the diagonal on a rowLater moments with the same shape: this comes back.
    Distance from the diagonalHow far apart the two moments are.
    The two halvesMirror images of each other: (i, j) and (j, i) are the same comparison.
    White line parallel to the diagonalA passage repeated later, at any pitch. The longer the line, the longer the repeat.
    Blue line parallel to the diagonalA passage repeated upside down.
    Blue line across the diagonalA passage played backwards.
    CheckerboardA stretch built from one shape and its mirror, such as scales running up and down.
    Dotted row or columnOne shape that keeps returning all through the piece.
    Square block on the diagonal (Seconds)A long held note: the same shape sounding for a while.

    The numbers

    NumberMeaning
    Same-shape pairsShare of all pairs of shapes that are white.
    Mirror pairsShare of all pairs that are blue.
    × chanceThe same count after shuffling the melody's own steps (seed 1). Same ingredients, random order. Above 1 means the order is doing something.
    Longest exact repeatThe longest white diagonal line, in notes: the longest passage that comes back exactly, at any pitch.
    Different shapesHow many different 5-note shapes the melody uses.
    Shapes inside our 256-spaceShapes whose steps are all ±1 or ±2 letters: the space Music from Symmetry composes in.

    What the picture keeps, and what it loses

    The picture is not one-to-one with the music. Many different melodies make the very same picture.

    InformationIn the picture?
    Which steps equal which, and which are mirrorsKept
    The exact pitchesLost: the same melody started on any note gives the same picture
    The size of each stepLost: doubling every step, or swapping steps of 1 and 2, gives the same picture
    Up and downLost: the melody played upside down gives the same picture
    The rhythmLost in Notes mode; the timing is kept in Seconds mode, but not the note lengths inside a shape
    Sharps and flatsLost in Letters mode, kept in Semitones mode
    Everything below the melodyLost: only the top line is used
    Backwards in timeKept: the melody played backwards gives the picture turned 180°

    Test: a decoder was given only the picture and rebuilt a melody from it. For 562 of 597 pieces (94%) the rebuilt melody makes a pixel-identical picture, yet only about 14% of its steps equal the real ones. For Mozart's K.545 the picture sorts all 584 steps into 49 groups; the exact notes need 49 more numbers (the size of each group's step), the first note, and the rhythm.

    What we found so far

    PieceResult
    Mozart, K.545, first movementSame-shape pairs 3.8%, about 20× chance; mirrors 4.0%, about 9× chance. The longest repeat is 75 notes: the opening returns at bar 42, a fourth higher, note for note. The scale runs make checkerboards.
    NoisePure noise, random walks and structured noise give scattered dots and no long lines. Their longest repeats are 4–11 notes, and they score about 1× chance.
    Music from Symmetry, draft 1Our rule repeats shapes locally (every move is a symmetry or a one-step change), but the notes it chose never repeat more than 32 in a row; its long repeats exist only where the large form was written by hand. Mozart is the opposite: free from moment to moment, repeating whole passages far apart.
    Beethoven, Moonlight Sonata, IIILongest exact repeat 194 notes, against 8 for chance.
