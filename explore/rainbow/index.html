Rainbow Vectors

Each wavelength of a rainbow becomes a 3-component RGB vector. Most of those vectors point outside the sRGB cube, so every on-screen rainbow is an approximation. Measure the loss here, then rebuild the spectrum from as few vectors as possible.

Primary bow, violet at 40.6° and red at 42.5° from the antisolar point. Each wavelength adds its light at its own angle and everything inside it, so the sky inside the bow glows and the band outside stays dark. Radial width exaggerated about 3×.

Method

    
Colour matching. CIE 1931 2° standard observer, tabulated every 5 nm and interpolated, then the standard XYZ → linear sRGB matrix (D65). Samples every 1 nm from 390 to 700 nm.

    
Gamut. A negative component means that wavelength is more saturated than any sRGB colour. Clip sets negatives to zero and shifts hue. Add white adds equal R, G and B until nothing is negative, which keeps the vector's direction in the chromaticity plane and costs saturation.

    
Bow angle. Water index from a Cauchy fit, n(λ) = 1.3240 + 3046/λ². Minimum-deviation angle for one internal reflection: cos² i = (n² − 1)/3, θ = 4r − 2i. Supernumerary bows need wave optics and are not modelled.

    
Metrics. ΔE00 is CIEDE2000 in CIELAB (D65). Turn angle is the plain Euclidean angle between consecutive linear RGB vectors, so it ignores how the eye weights hues.
