Classification of finite simple groups

Periodic Table of Finite Simple Groups

Every finite group is assembled from simple groups, the way molecules are assembled from atoms. Each group's list of building blocks is unique (the Jordan–Hölder theorem), and the classification theorem, whose proof was completed in 2004, says this page holds all of them: 18 infinite families and 26 sporadic groups.

Tap any tile for its order, factorization, members and its rule: the generators and relations that create it. The table scrolls sideways.

Notation: An(q) = PSLn+1(q), Bn(q) = Ω2n+1(q), Cn(q) = PSp2n(q), Dn(q) = PΩ2n+(q), 2An(q2) = PSUn+1(q), 2Dn(q2) = PΩ2n−(q). Here q is a prime power. Twisted families sit in the row of their untwisted diagram's rank.

Every order, factorization and lookup is computed exactly in your browser with big-integer arithmetic. The lookup checks every family at every rank and field size, so a "no" is a theorem, not a guess.

Rules: the sporadic presentations are from the ATLAS of Group Representations; the families use the presentations of Steinberg, Carmichael, and Behr and Mennicke, and Conway’s Y-diagrams. “Checked here” means verified with the GAP computer algebra system when this page was built.
