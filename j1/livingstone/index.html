How to read it

Each vertex has 11 neighbours, split into back (closer to the root), same distance, and forward. These counts depend only on the distance, never on which vertex you pick or which vertex is the root. That is distance-transitivity, and the table above holds for every one of the 266 choices.

Every button applies an element of J1. The dots fly to new places, but the drawing they land in is the same drawing, because a symmetry sends edges to edges. Spin uses an element of order 11 that fixes the root. Press it 11 times and every vertex is back home. It also fixes exactly one of the 12 far vertices, drawn in the corner.

Construction: J1 generated by Janko's two 7×7 matrices over 𝔽₁₁ (orders 7 and 5), acting on 1,540 projective points, then on the 266 conjugates of a PSL(2,11) subgroup. The graph is the orbital of valency 11. Layer sizes and neighbour counts were verified by search, not assumed.
